    How the numbers work

    Team ratings

    Each team gets an attack and a defence rating from goals for and against per game. Last season's rates, pulled 40% toward the league average, count as 15 games of evidence. This season's results are added on top, so the ratings shift more with every game played. A newly promoted team starts slightly below average.

    Season simulation

    Every remaining game is played out with Poisson-distributed goals and a small home-ice edge. Ties go to overtime, won in proportion to team strength. Points follow SHL rules: 3 for a regulation win, 2 for an OT or shootout win, 1 for an OT or shootout loss. After 52 games, 1–6 go to the quarterfinals, 7–10 play best-of-three play-ins, and 13–14 face the relegation playoff.

    Gold odds

    The playoffs are simulated too. Play-in winners meet seeds 1 and 2, the bracket reseeds after each round, and every series from the quarterfinals on is best of seven. Gold % is the share of simulations a team wins the final.

    Impact rating

    The SHL doesn't publish shot-location or on-ice data, so this isn't WAR. It's a weighted blend of percentile ranks in points/60, ice time, plus-minus, shots, finishing, power-play goals, hits, blocks and penalty minutes, compared within forwards or defencemen. This season's games count 1.5× as much as last season's.

    GSAA

    Goals saved above average = saves − shots faced × league save %. It doesn't adjust for shot quality, so a goalie behind a leaky defence can look worse than they are.

    Data

    Scores, standings and box stats come from shl.se's public stats feeds. Team colours are approximations used for the badges. SHLstats is an independent fan project with no connection to the SHL or its clubs.
